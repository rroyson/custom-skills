# Session Playing

How a hired player adds one part to a finished track. Distilled from working players and arrangers (Carlton, Cropper, Gadd, Jamerson, Porcaro, Hood, Blaine, Hey, Kaye, Owsinski); sources in `research/session-musician.md`. The rules; SKILL.md is the order.

## 1. The listening pass
Play nothing on the first run-through. Chart it the way a Nashville player charts a demo in one listen:
- **Key and mode.** Tempo and the subdivision the groove lives on: straight eighths, sixteenths, shuffle, half-time.
- **Form** with bar counts, and which sections lift.
- **The vocal:** register (low, mid, high for that voice), phrase length, and where it breathes: the bar at the end of a line, the gap before a chorus, the turnaround.
- **Ownership.** Each existing track owns a register and a rhythm. Bass owns the low end. Strummed guitar owns mid chords on eighths. An arp owns the sixteenth grid. Drums own the backbeat. Write it down per track.
- **The hole:** a register nobody fills, a rhythm nobody plays against, a section that is thinner than the one before it. The part goes in the hole. Build it off the vocal melody and the lyric, not the chord chart.

## 2. Roles
One role per part. Name the role before the instrument.

| Role | What it does | Pick it when | Rhythm |
|---|---|---|---|
| bed (pad) | one sustained note or chord under everything | nothing sustains; verses feel thin; the mix has no glue | whole notes, one swell per bar or per chord |
| answer (fill) | a short phrase in the vocal's breaths | the vocal leaves gaps at line ends; the song needs a second character | four beats at most, finished before the next sung line |
| hook | the line the listener hums; intro, turnaround, outro | the intro is empty; the song has no signature | a two-bar motif, repeated exactly, in the vocal's absence |
| rhythm | plays against the foundation: offbeat chank, shaker, chop, clav | nothing moves against the groove; the track feels flat, not empty | offbeats, backbeats, or a 16th grid nobody else has |
| double | the same line as an existing part, new timbre or octave | the last chorus needs weight; a hook needs to be bigger | matches the doubled part exactly |
| texture | intermittent color: a swell, a reversed note, a chime | the track is complete and needs air, not notes | once per section, then silence |
| transition | a pickup, walk-up, or cascade at a seam | sections butt together with no lead-in | the last bar or two before the seam |

Ceiling: four elements sounding at once in the densest section (foundation, pad, rhythm, lead), fills in the gaps. A fifth is mud.

Play the song, not the instrument. Nobody buys a solo. Simplest that works; bring an optional second figure, never a busier first one.

## 3. Arrangement rules
- **While the singer sings:** long notes, low level, or nothing. Never the melody. Never the vocal's register.
- **Fills live in the breaths.** Four beats max, done before the next phrase. One fill instrument at a time.
- **Move against the vocal.** Long notes when the vocal is busy, motion when the vocal holds. Contrary or oblique motion; parallel motion disappears into the vocal.
- **Register by what is unowned.** Above the guitar chords, below the vocal, never the bass's lane. The octave around middle C is the vocal's; high pads pull the ear off the melody.
- **Earn the entrance.** Hold the part until verse 2, the pre-chorus, or chorus 2. Leave the first chorus room to grow. A part that plays from bar one is a bed, and only a bed.
- **One change per section.** Add or remove one element every section. The last chorus gets the double or the countermelody. Harmonies on the title line, not the whole chorus.
- **Signature moves are hooks, not wallpaper.** Swell, chank, stab, chop, Leslie switch: once per section.
- **Leave space.** Something can happen in a space. Take a breath.
- **Feel over correctness.** The slightly-behind first take is often the record.
- **Bring an idea.** Not a clone of a famous player; the sound described, then the part that only this song needs.

## 4. Instrument idioms
Role it does best · home register · signature moves · avoid.

| Instrument | Best role | Register | Moves | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| lap steel | bed, answer, texture | mid | slow slides into the note, volume swells, tremolo picking, behind-the-bar mutes, edge-of-breakup amp, spring reverb; slowed down and breathing it sits like a string quartet | busy licks under lyric; the chord bends only pedal steel can do |
| pedal steel | crying bed, answer, intro hook | mid-high, above the guitar chords | violining swell (volume pedal off, strike, open), pedal bends (one chord tone moves while the others hold), bar slides into the nut, palm harmonics, bar-roll vibrato | continuous playing under the vocal; stacking fills on the fiddle's |
| dobro | answer, break, chop | mid | bar slides, hammer-ons, rolls, sharp attacks | muddy low register; doubling banjo rolls |
| slide guitar | answer, hook | mid-high | open tuning, glass slide, slides in and out of pitch like a voice, wide vibrato | lines across the lyric; string noise |
| Hammond B3 | bed, chorus swell, gospel stab | mid, under the vocal | Leslie slow in the verse and fast at the chorus lift, switched mid-phrase; drawbars filling in as the song builds; stab plus gliss for R&B; swell into the chorus | full chords in the vocal register all song; fast Leslie everywhere |
| Rhodes | comp bed, intro figure, ostinato | mid | tine bell tone, rolling arpeggios, tremolo pan | both hands in the bass register; exact piano doubles |
| Wurlitzer | rhythm comp, riff | mid | short chords dug in so it barks, space between phrases, built-in tremolo | sustained pads (it isn't one) |
| piano | foundation comp, intro hook, pickup runs | left hand octaves and fifths, right hand above middle C | arpeggiated eighths, walk-up into the chorus, a high sparkle line | left-hand root fighting the bass; block chords in the vocal range |
| horns (soul, funk) | stab, hook, chorus lift | trumpets top, trombones under | close-voiced staccato stabs, pushes into the downbeat, falls and doits, unison for punch; pads under verses, fills in breaks, prominent voicings in the chorus | lines across the vocal; high sustained brass |
| horns (jazz, big band) | background, swell | same | drop-2 voicings, sectional swells behind the lead | busy lines under the melody |
| strings | bed, counter-line, hits | cellos on root and fifth, inner voices around middle C, violins in octaves above the vocal | straight slow whole and half notes against a syncopated vocal, swell on a held chord, tremolo, pizzicato pulse, staccato hits, a descant that is extremely simple | high pads under the vocal |
| cello | low counter-line, pulse | below the vocal | sustained root and fifth, pizzicato pulse, a riff ostinato entering after phrases end | competing with a low male vocal; mimicking the vocal contour |
| fiddle | answer, long-bow bed, chop | above the vocal | long-bow double-stops, two or three notes in a breath, chop when nobody covers the backbeat | the melody under the vocal; doubling steel fills |
| mandolin | chop, tremolo bed (ballads), kick-off | high | chop on two and four, tremolo, crosspicked fills, slides | chop plus banjo roll plus fiddle chop at once |
| banjo | roll drive, four-beat fills, vamp | high | forward rolls, licks in the breaths | anything continuous under a vocal; its attack competes with words |
| harmonica | answer, intro and turnaround hook | high-mid | cross harp, bends, warbles, train chug, cupped for grit; fills between lines | playing while the singer sings; heavy compression |
| acoustic guitar layer | rhythm bed, shimmer double | mid-high | Nashville high-strung tuning doubling the main acoustic for a 12-string shimmer; capo up for a second voicing; fingerpicked arpeggio for intimacy | a second acoustic in the same voicing and register |
| electric guitar | rhythm chank, arpeggio bed, answer licks, ambient swell | mid | chank (choked offbeat or backbeat chord), clean delayed eighth-note arpeggio, chicken pickin' and double-stops in the gaps, volume or feedback swells with the attack cut into reverb | lead across the lyric; distorted chords in the bass register |
| synth pad | bed | high-passed, above the bass | root and fifth low in sparse sections, fuller up top in choruses, slow filter opening | filling the vocal's octave |
| synth arp | rhythm, shimmer | mid-high | a grid nobody else owns; slower arp to fill gaps in a busy track; velocity ducked on kick and snare | doubling a grid already on the timeline |
| synth lead | counter-melody, hook | above the vocal | mono, portamento, one motif, dotted-eighth delay | more than one motif |
| backing vocals | bed (oohs), answer, title-line stack | around the lead | "ooh" for quiet, "ah" for lift; hold pitch when the lead moves; answer phrases in the gaps; wide | a full stack across the whole chorus |
| shaker, tambourine, congas | rhythm | n/a | shaker sixteenths for air, tambourine on the backbeat to brighten the snare, tumbao shifted into the spaces; up a notch at the bridge | a layer that changes the groove |
| bass (upright) | foundation | low | round, quick decay; walk-ups; slap for rockabilly | sustain it doesn't have |
| bass (electric) | foundation, hook | low | flatwounds and foam mute for the classic thump; ghost notes, chromatic walk-ups, syncopation built off the vocal melody | fighting the drums |
| sax, clarinet, flute | answer, soft counter-melody, break solo | sax mid, clarinet low, flute high | long tones under chords, fills in the gaps, a solo in the break; flute trills and accents over rhythmic chords; clarinet as a warm low pad | stepping on the vocal |

## 5. Vocabulary for the prompt
Words the model can act on. Pair each with a note value or a place in the bar.

swell (crescendo from silence on a held note; violining on steel) · pad / bed (a sustained note or chord under everything) · stab / hit (one staccato chord, usually on beat one) · chank / skank (choked offbeat or backbeat chord) · comp (chords and rhythms supporting the melody) · ostinato / riff / vamp (a repeated figure) · motif / hook (the short idea the listener keeps) · answer phrase (a response after the vocal's call) · pickup (notes leading into a downbeat) · turnaround (the bars that lead back or forward at a seam) · pedal tone / drone (one note held while the chords move) · walk-up / walk-down (stepwise line into the next chord) · cascade (a figure falling through registers) · gliss / smear (slide between pitches) · harmonic / chime (bell overtone) · ghost note (rhythm without pitch) · push (hit an eighth early) · tremolo (rapid repeat of one note) · chop (percussive backbeat chord on fiddle or mandolin) · double-stop · chicken pickin' · Leslie slow / fast · behind-the-nut bend · long bows · pizzicato · staccato · fall / doit / shake (horn articulations) · unison / harmonized · drop-2 · descant (a high simple counter-line) · oblique / contrary motion.

## 6. Judging the two takes
Studio returns two takes per generation. Listen for, in this order:
1. **Time.** Generated parts have landed ahead of the beat; nudge the clip or regenerate. Feel a hair behind is fine; ahead is not.
2. **Bleed.** Extra instruments or a vocal in an instrument take. Thumbs-down "multiple stems" and regenerate with the instrument named more plainly.
3. **The vocal.** Any note that lands on a sung word is the wrong note. Reword the rhythm sentence so the part rests while the vocal moves.
4. **The register.** If it sits in the vocal's or the bass's octave, say the octave in the prompt ("an octave above the vocal", "above the guitar chords").
5. **Density.** Too many notes: state the note value and the rests ("whole notes", "a two-bar phrase, then two bars of rest"). Too few: add the signature move, once per section.
6. **The instrument.** Wrong timbre: give the fuller name and the gear ("lap steel through a small tube amp, spring reverb"), or the neighbor instrument that Suno hears more reliably.
Time signature is not sent to the model; odd meters need the rhythm spelled in the prompt.

## 7. Parts that made the record
Organ on "Like a Rolling Stone": pad plus answers, an eighth behind the band, turned up · pedal steel on "Teach Your Children": counter-line and fills, first take kept · Rhodes on "Riders on the Storm": ostinato as texture, rain on glass · strings on "Eleanor Rigby": staccato chords as the whole rhythm section · horns on "Vehicle": the intro stab is the identity · clavinet on "Superstition": two interlocking rhythm parts panned apart · ambient steel on Lanois's "Amazing Grace": soft attacks and echo merged into one line · harmonica on "Heart of Gold": intro and turnaround hook, the only color; the steel on two strings · strings on "Bitter Sweet Symphony": a looped four-bar figure the song is built on · "Green Onions": flat organ riff, guitar squawks in answer, backbeat and nothing else · steel intro on "Night Life": the intro and the solo bridges are the record.
