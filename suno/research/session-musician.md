# Session musician thinking: adding one part to a finished track

Purpose: decision rules and vocabulary for a skill that prompts an AI music tool to generate a single added part ("lap steel on this", "B3 under the chorus"). Tags: (primary) = interview/book/masterclass by a working session player, producer, or arranger; (secondary) = blog, forum, summary, or press paraphrase.

## 1. The listening pass

- Don't play on the first run-through. Larry Carlton: "There are a lot of guitar players that play along on the first run-through of a track, because they want to make sure that they are going to find something, so they can be heard. But my approach was always: don't even play on the first run through, just listen to the song. We have time." https://www.musicradar.com/news/guitars/larry-carlton-on-steely-dan-joni-mitchell-and-solo-secrets-626839 (primary)
- Listen to everyone, then play around it. Steve Cropper (AP, 2020): "I listen to the other musicians and the singer. I'm not listening to just me... Once we've presented the song, then I listen to the song and the way they interpret it. And I play around all that stuff. That's what I do. That's my style." https://www.cbsnews.com/news/steve-cropper-dies-age-84-guitarist-songwriter-booker-t-and-the-m-g-s/ (secondary, quoting AP)
- Ears before eyes. Steve Gadd: "You can't put your eyes before your ears. The key thing that tells you what's happening, is listening." https://mydrumlessons.co.uk/2017/07/playin-for-the-music/ (secondary, quoting Gadd)
- Build the part from the vocal melody, not the chart. James Jamerson: "When they gave me that chord sheet, I'd look at it, but then start doing what I felt... I'd hear the melody line from the lyrics and I'd build the bass line around that." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Jamerson (secondary, quoting Jamerson)
- Arranger's first pass = take off the form. Jerry Hey: "As I'm listening for the first time and taking off the form of the record, if anything comes to mind, I'll write it down." Goal: "try to make the horns fit like they were supposed to be there in the first place." https://daily.redbullmusicacademy.com/2016/05/interview-jerry-hey/ (primary)
- Nashville Number System = the one-listen chart. Players "chart out an entire song on one piece of paper while hearing a demo of the tune for the first time." Chart holds chords as scale degrees (1-4-5-1), section form, and rhythm marks (diamond = "strike and hold a chord", pushes, splits); same chart works in any key. Harold Bradley: it "saved you 15 minutes a session if you could do the charts before the session." https://nashvillenumbersystem.com/introduction/ (primary, Chas Williams); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashville_Number_System (secondary)
- A-Team was hired to "learn new material quickly, play it flawlessly, and avoid overplaying so as to showcase the vocalist." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Nashville_A-Team (secondary)
- Wrecking Crew worked from chord charts + demo only. Carol Kaye: "We never had lead sheets... We had chord charts only. We'd hear the singer sing the song or we listened to a demo." https://forbassplayersonly.com/interview-carol-kaye/ (primary)
- Muscle Shoals built outward from drums and bass. Jimmy Johnson: "Our sound was built around the drum and bass. We just had to find the groove." "In Muscle Shoals we did head sessions where everyone contributed." https://thesouthernrambler.com/ramblin-with-the-swampers-of-muscle-shoals/ (secondary, quoting Johnson)
- Bar between vocal phrases is the target. "When there's a bar between vocal phrases that could use some decoration, a response from a supporting musician might be appropriate." https://www.saxontheweb.net/threads/accompanying-singers.351430/ (secondary)
- Listening-pass checklist (synthesis of above): key/mode; tempo and subdivision (straight 8ths, 16ths, shuffle, half-time); form with bar counts; vocal register, phrase length, where it breathes; who owns low end, mid chords, top sparkle, backbeat, off-beat; the holes: gaps between vocal phrases, turnarounds, intro/outro, pre-chorus lift, last chorus.

## 2. Role selection

- The five arrangement elements (Owsinski): Foundation = "the bass and drums, but can also include a rhythm guitar and/or keys if they're playing the same rhythmic figure"; Pad = "a long sustaining note or chord... a Hammond Organ provided the best pad"; Rhythm = "any instrument that plays counter to the Foundation element... a double time shaker or tambourine, a rhythm guitar strumming on the backbeat, or congas"; Lead = "a lead vocal, lead instrument or solo"; Fills = "occur in the spaces between Lead lines, or can be a signature line... an answer to the Lead." Ceiling: "Usually there should not be more than four arrangement elements playing at the same time. Sometimes three elements can work very well. Very rarely will five simultaneous elements work together." https://bobbyowsinskiblog.com/song-arrangement-elements/ (primary, producer/author)
- Map of requested roles to elements: pad/bed = Pad; counter-melody = second Lead (long notes vs active vocal); answer/fill = Fills; rhythmic hook/riff = Rhythm (or Foundation if it locks with bass/drums); doubling = reinforces an existing element, adds none; bass reinforcement = Foundation; texture/ear candy = intermittent Pad or Fill; intro/outro hook = Lead while vocal is absent; turnaround/transition = Fill at the section seam.
- Pick by what is missing: nothing sustains -> Pad; nothing moves against the groove -> Rhythm; vocal leaves gaps -> Fills; intro empty -> signature line; then check the four-element ceiling in the densest section. (Derived from Owsinski above.)
- Producers hire part-makers. Steve Lukather: "That's the kind of player people want to hire in the studio – the person who comes up with parts that make the song feel better." "You need to know how to play behind a singer, and to have great time, a great sound, and a great touch." Paid "to play rhythm guitar 90 per cent of the time." https://www.guitarplayer.com/players/steve-lukather-rhythm-guitar (primary)
- "People Don't Buy a Guitar Solo. They Buy a Song." (Lukather, 1984) https://www.guitarplayer.com/players/people-dont-buy-a-guitar-solo-they-buy-a-song-steve-lukather-talks-soloing-songwriting-and-success (primary)
- Non-players hear only groove and time. Jeff Porcaro: "They only know groove and time. They don't know that you have a nice wrist..." "You'd better just play time. Period! Really good time. And make it tasty. And have dynamics, listen to the lyrics of the song, and be there as a timekeeper." "If someone calls on you to pull a trick or two out of your hat, then cool. But basically... they're saying, 'Dig how cool that groove is.'" http://jeffporcaro.blogspot.com/2006/12/drum-interview.html (primary, 1991 DRUM! interview repost)
- No initials. Steve Gadd: "I try to play the music without putting my own initials on it." "To play as simply and as unnoticed as I could became as challenging as playing at a high energy level." "Sometimes it's better not to have drums." https://mydrumlessons.co.uk/2017/07/playin-for-the-music/ (secondary, quoting Gadd)
- Fewer notes. David Hood: "I'm more comfortable playing less." "You're not playing for yourself or for other bass players – you're playing to make a song come out." "Usually it helps to simplify." "I don't fight with [drummers]." https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/articles/david-hood-aretha-otis-redding-120000962.html (primary, Guitar World syndication)
- One shot. Hal Blaine: "One good shot in the right place in a song is worth a million paradiddles." "We used to go for 'feel' as opposed to robotic performance." https://www.musicradar.com/artists/drummers/hal-blaine-drum-week (primary)
- Space is generative. Carlton: "you play something and you leave a space. Well, a number of things can happen in the space." "Sax players have to take a breath... So take a breath." https://www.musicradar.com/news/guitars/larry-carlton-on-steely-dan-joni-mitchell-and-solo-secrets-626839 (primary)
- Bring ideas, not clones. Grady Martin to Lloyd Green: "Lloyd, if we wanted Emmons or Day, we'd hire them! You got a thousand ideas floatin' around in your head, if you'll just relax and play 'em!" https://www.vintageguitar.com/3683/lloyd-green/ (primary)
- Producers often give zero direction, so the player self-selects the role. Kaye on Quincy Jones: "Just play anything you want." Hey: "They have me do what I think is best"; he brings "an optional chorus figure, maybe two." (Kaye, Hey URLs above)
- Signature intros were the job. Reggie Young: "That's what you used to do to make records sound different." "Son of a Preacher Man" intro "is sort of a Chet Atkins lick, because it uses an open string." "I was playing more melodies than chord changes." https://www.premierguitar.com/artists/forgotten-heroes-reggie-young (primary)
- Minimalism is confidence. On "Green Onions": "Only the best musicians are wise and confident enough to play this minimally." https://ethanhein.substack.com/p/green-onions (secondary). Cropper: "I figured the world didn't need another Chet Atkins, didn't need another Les Paul, didn't need another B.B. King." https://www.songfacts.com/facts/booker-t-the-mgs/green-onions (secondary)
- Muscle Shoals asked for "a lick", not "this lick". Jimmy Johnson: "Very few people came up to Spooner and said, 'Give me this lick.' They said, 'Spooner, give me a lick.'" (Southern Rambler URL above)
- Easy Eye Sound: Auerbach's band (Bobby Wood, Gene Chrisman, Russ Pahl, Dave Roe) cuts live; "the way records are made at Easy Eye Sound reminds them of how they used to make records with Memphis legend Chips Moman." https://clevelandmagazine.com/articles/dan-auerbach-brings-all-star-session-musicians-to-town/ (secondary)
- Leisz described as "a zen master who dwells in the deep, almost imperceptible background," making "painterly washes of sound, with no beginning and no end point." https://echolocator.substack.com/p/following-elusive-breadcrumbs-greg (secondary). No usable Leisz, Franklin, or Ben Folds session quote found in fetchable sources.
- Tedesco: "Bring a pencil." https://bb.steelguitarforum.com/viewtopic.php?t=312386 (secondary). "The complete sellable product is your sound, don't blow it." https://hinj-noiz.com/2022/10/17/bookshelf-for-guitar-players-only-tommy-tedesco/ (secondary, quoting the book)

## 3. Arrangement rules of thumb

- Fill only in the breaths, four beats max. "A four-beat fill is the practical unit of vocal backup. It's long enough to say something musical. However, it's short enough to be completely out of the way before the singer's next phrase begins." Role is "complementary, not continuous." https://blog.artistworks.com/vocal-backup-banjo-fill-holes/ (secondary)
- Never the melody under the vocal; one fill instrument at a time. "No instrument should play the melody during vocals." "One chopping instrument at a time." "It's okay to not play at all for long periods of time." https://www.fiddlehangout.com/archive/42347 (secondary)
- Don't step on the vocal. "Don't step on the vocals. When the singer is singing, they should be heard." "If in doubt play nothing, or some counterpoint." https://cafesaxophone.com/threads/playing-with-a-vocalist.36144/ (secondary)
- Under the vocal, simplify and drop volume. "If you want to play while they sing, play very simple riffs or chords and bring your volume down." https://www.harmonicalessons.com/when_in.html (secondary)
- Countermelody rhythm rule: "when your main melody is active, you'll want the countermelody to be comprised of longer notes that don't get in the way, and when the main melody is rhythmically static, you'll want the countermelody to step up." In pop the countermelody "will be used toward the end of the song, as a way of increasing song energy." https://www.secretsofsongwriting.com/2011/11/07/5-steps-to-writing-an-effective-countermelody/ (secondary)
- Contrary/oblique motion separates lines: "Contrary motion is used to distinguish individual lines from one another." "Just holding a note in one voice to create oblique motion can add fluidity." https://blog.landr.com/counterpoint/ (secondary)
- Strings: "high, soaring violins line, often written as a counterpoint to the vocal melody"; write "straight, slow on-beat whole and half notes" against syncopated vocals; descants "can be extremely simple." https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/arranging-strings-part-4 (primary, Dave Stewart)
- Register: "The octave surrounding middle C is neutral territory... and it's in the vocal range. Pads in the high register can draw attention away from the melody." https://www.berklee.edu/berklee-today/fall-2018/writing-string-pads (secondary). "Violins share a lot of their register with higher vocal ranges, and cellos with lower ones." https://theproaudiofiles.com/adding-string-arrangements-to-your-music/ (secondary)
- Horns by section: intro = section rhythmic figure; verses = "pads under vocals; supportive"; breaks = "horn fills between vocal phrases"; chorus = "more prominent voicings." "For fast moving lines keeping the voices tighter works best. For slow moving pads keeping the voices spread works best." https://hornarrangements.com/basicshornarranging.html (primary, working arranger)
- Contrast per section: "every section should feel different from the one before it... adding or removing elements." https://songen.app/blog/how-to-arrange-a-pop-song/ (secondary). Add a new element every 8 bars to keep it evolving. https://www.pressreader.com/australia/future-music-9629/20170629/282041917130241 (secondary)
- Leave the first chorus room: "Making the first chorus a stripped down chorus is a cool way to bring power into a chorus with dynamics." https://www.toughtones.com/better-chorus-with-arrangement/ (secondary). "Keep one or two layers smaller before the hook arrives." https://www.sunopromptpro.com/en/guides/suno-chorus-prompts (secondary)
- Energy arc: "verse pulls them in, pre-chorus lifts, chorus delivers, bridge resets, and final chorus pays off everything that came before." https://orphiq.com/resources/parts-of-a-song-explained (secondary)
- Stacks don't run the whole chorus: "bringing harmonies in on the second half, or only on the title line, keeps the arrangement dynamic." https://violetrecording.com/how-to-record-backing-vocals/ (secondary)
- Percussion earns its layer: "only add layers that improve the groove"; shaker 16ths add "constant movement and air"; tambourine on 2 and 4 "reinforcing the snare hits." https://www.pointblankmusicschool.com/blog/how-to-master-percussion-layering-for-richer-beats/ (secondary)
- Pads stay behind: "pads usually stay in the background to elevate more leading vocals or instruments"; "in a dense arrangement, your pad should primarily serve to fill out gaps in the frequency spectrum." https://lunacy.audio/news/synth-pad/ ; https://emastered.com/blog/synth-pad (secondary)
- Feel wins over the "correct" take: Nash kept Garcia's spontaneous first take on "Teach Your Children" over the second take Garcia asked for. https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/the-first-time-graham-nash-met-jerry-garcia/ (secondary)

## 4. Instrument idiom cheat sheet

Format: role / register / signature moves / avoid.

- Pedal steel: crying pad, answer fills, intro or solo hook / mid-high, above guitar chords / volume swell ("strike the strings with the volume pedal almost all the way off... then slowly depress" = "violining"), pedal bends (bend one note of a chord while others hold), bar slides into and off the nut, slants, palm harmonics/chimes, bar-roll vibrato, blocking / continuous playing under the vocal; fills stacked on top of fiddle fills. https://steelguitaracademy.com/instruction/techniques/ (secondary); https://www.premierguitar.com/lessons/twang-101-pedal-steel-bends-101 (primary lesson); Ben Keith on "Heart of Gold" is "simple, effective and brilliant... a study of restraint," mostly strings 3 and 5 with A/B pedals https://bb.steelguitarforum.com/viewtopic.php?t=126460 (secondary)
- Lap steel: atmosphere, blues/rock slide fills, Hawaiian/Americana color / mid / "glassy, sustaining... brighter or edgier"; suits amp overdrive; slow slides, swells, tremolo picking, behind-the-bar dead notes / pedal-steel chord bends it can't do; busy licks under lyric. https://fretterverse.com/dobro-vs-steel-guitar-comparison/ ; https://playslideguitar.com/lap-steel-guitar-and-dobro-differences/ (secondary). Lanois approach: slow the steel down, let it breathe, think of it "like a string quartet" (paraphrase) https://www.uncut.co.uk/features/shimmering-synths-pedal-steel-the-healing-power-of-ambient-americana-154910/ (secondary)
- Dobro: bluegrass/Americana fills, breaks, chop / mid / "punchy, warm... sharp attacks for rhythmic clarity and melodic breaks"; bar slides, open-position hammer/pull, rolls / doubling banjo rolls; muddy low register. https://acousticworld.net/dobro-vs-pedal-steel-vs-lap-steel-whats-the-difference/ (secondary)
- Slide guitar: vocal-like lead fills, hook riff / mid-high / open E or G, glass slide, fingerpick with fingers muting unplayed strings for "clarity and precision," "slides in and out of pitch to create a sound like a human voice," wide vibrato / string noise; lines across the lyric. https://www.guitarworld.com/magazine/slidedog-slide-guitar-mastery-duane-allman-video (secondary)
- Hammond B3: the classic Pad; swell under chorus; gospel stabs and glisses / mid, below vocal / Leslie slow in verse, fast on chorus lift, switch mid-phrase; drawbars 888000000 "power" for rock, 00 8000 000 "understated, haunting" for mellow, 88 0000 220 (Charles Hodges) soul with C3 chorus and fast Leslie; Booker T tip: "add some C3-setting chorus vibrato to your fast Leslie passages"; "use stabs and glissandos together for R&B"; add drawbars as the song builds / full chords in the vocal register all song; fast Leslie everywhere. https://www.musicradar.com/how-to/how-to-get-that-classic-rock-organ-sound-on-a-hammond-b-3 (secondary)
- Wurlitzer / Rhodes: comp bed, riff, intro figure / mid / Rhodes = tine, "smooth, warm," bell-like, rolling arpeggios; Wurli = reed, "punchy, sharp," barks when dug in, "encouraging shorter chords, digging into keys harder, and leaving more space between phrases," built-in tremolo / both hands in bass register; exact piano doubles. https://gearspace.com/threads/rhodes-or-wurly.944718/ ; https://reverb.com/news/rhodes-vs-wurlitzer-comparing-classic-electric-pianos (secondary)
- Piano: Foundation comp or Lead intro figure / LH octaves-fifths, RH above middle C / "Your left hand is like the bass player... if everyone is busy all the time, it just sounds messy"; know the bass part and "voice chords appropriately and avoid clashes"; arpeggiated 8ths, pickup runs into chorus, high sparkle / LH root fighting bass; RH block chords in vocal range. https://www.pianote.com/blog/left-hand-patterns/ ; https://tamingthesaxophone.com/theory/arranging/jazz-piano-guitar (secondary)
- Horn section: soul/funk = "staccato delivery is an essential part of soul and funk phrasing," close-voiced harmonised stabs, pushes into downbeats, falls/doits/shakes, unison for punch; jazz/big band = drop-2, backgrounds behind soloist, sectional swells; mariachi = trumpets in parallel thirds, wide vibrato, long held notes (convention; no source found) / trumpets top, bones under / pads on high brass (endurance); lines across the vocal. https://hornarrangements.com/basicshornarranging.html (primary); https://www.evanrogersmusic.com/blog-contents/big-band-arranging/writing-horns-for-pop-songs (primary)
- String section / quartet: pad, counter-line, staccato hits / celli on "root and possibly the fifth down in the bass clef," other tones "in the alto range around middle C," violins in octaves above the vocal / swell on held chord, tremolo, pizzicato, staccato stabs, portamento / high pads that "draw attention away from the melody." https://www.berklee.edu/berklee-today/fall-2018/writing-string-pads ; https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/arranging-strings-part-4
- Fiddle: fills in vocal breaths, long-bow pads, chop / above vocal / "long-bow double-stop chords or simple long-bow chord tones"; "chop when nobody else is covering the backbeat"; "two or three notes often say everything" / melody under the vocal; doubling steel fills. https://www.fiddlehangout.com/archive/42347 (secondary)
- Mandolin: chop on 2 and 4, tremolo pad on ballads, crosspicked fills, kick-off / high / chop, tremolo, slides / chop + banjo roll + fiddle chop simultaneously. https://blog.artistworks.com/mandolin-solo-backup-chop-roll-banjo/ ; https://pickinbluegrass.com/mandolin/ (secondary)
- Banjo: roll drive, four-beat fills, vamp under vocal / high / forward rolls, licks in breaths / "The banjo's percussive attack naturally competes with vocals, making restraint your most important tool." https://blog.artistworks.com/vocal-backup-banjo-fill-holes/ (secondary)
- Harmonica: fills, intro/turnaround hook / high-mid / cross harp (2nd position) for blues/rock, bends, warbles, train chug, hand-cupped mic for grit; "play in the 'fill' areas between vocal lines" / over-compression: "so much of its passion is in its dynamics." https://www.harmonicalessons.com/when_in.html ; https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/capturing-perfect-harmonica-recordings (secondary)
- Acoustic guitar layer: strummed bed (Rhythm) or shimmer double / Nashville (high-strung) tuning replaces low four strings with octave-up strings; "doubling a standard-tuned acoustic part with a Nashville-strung guitar creates a wide 12-string shimmer"; capo for a second voicing; fingerpicked arpeggio for intimacy / two acoustics, same voicing, same register. https://guitarwiz.app/articles/nashville-tuning-guitar/ (secondary)
- Electric guitar: chank/skank = "a short, staccato chord hit on the off-beats"; funk chank = choked 16th strums; arpeggio = clean delayed 8ths; lead = answer licks in gaps, chicken pickin', double-stops, pedal-steel-style bends; ambient = volume/feedback swells "cutting the initial attack of the note" into reverb/delay / lead across the lyric; distorted chords in the bass register. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chanking ; https://bassculture.substack.com/p/the-reggae-guitar-skank ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volume_swell (secondary); Lukather rhythm 90% (primary, above)
- Synth lead / arp / pad: pad "connects, adds depth to, or fills space"; high-pass it; "drop the upper tones and reinforce the root and fifth in a lower register" in sparse sections; arp faster = shimmer, slower "can be used to fill a few gaps in an already busy arrangement," duck velocity on kick/snare; lead = mono, portamento, one motif. https://lunacy.audio/news/synth-pad/ ; https://blog.native-instruments.com/arpeggiators/ (secondary)
- Backing vocals / oohs: "ooooh" for quiet moments, "oh"/"aah" for high energy; if the lead melody moves a lot, BGVs "stay more constant in pitch"; answer phrases in the gaps; double and pan wide; drop consonants for warmth / full stack across the whole chorus. https://blog.airgigs.com/2017/02/the-anatomy-of-a-background-vocal-arrangement-part-2-bgvs/ ; https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/crafting-perfect-pop-backing-vocals (secondary)
- Percussion: shaker 16ths (air), tambourine on backbeat (brightness), congas tumbao: "lock in with the drum set groove, and shift the pattern into the spaces"; "at a bridge section you can take it up a notch" / adding layers that don't change the groove. https://www.drummercafe.com/education/lessons/congas-in-rock-pop-music-son-tumbao.html ; pointblank URL above (secondary)
- Bass (upright vs electric): upright = "slower, rounder attack and fade away more quickly," warmer, for jazz/folk/rockabilly slap; electric = sustain and control / Kaye: P-Bass, flatwounds, pick, foam mute = "the sound all the producers wanted" https://www.guitarworld.com/features/carol-kaye-interview-2023 (primary); Jamerson: chromatic runs, syncopation, ghost notes, open strings, built around the vocal melody (Wikipedia above); Hood: upbeat placement learned from Otis Redding / fighting the drummer. https://www.premierguitar.com/electric-bass-upright-sound (secondary)
- Cello: low counter-line, sustained root/fifth, pizzicato pulse, riff ostinato / "sustained, arpeggiated line placed lower than the lead, entering after phrases end and never mimicking the vocal contour" / competing with a low male vocal. https://www.versetuned.com/how-to-write-a-counter-melody-a-practitioners-guide-to-counter-melody-vs-counterpoint-with-5-pop-examples-a-10-minute-worksheet/ (secondary)
- Flute / clarinet / sax: sax = fills in gaps, "soft countermelodies as part of background chords," solo in the break, long tones; flute "works nicely on top of rhythmic piano or guitar chords, with trills, accents, or mellow guide lines in lower register"; clarinet = warm low-register pad or klezmer/trad color / stepping on the vocal; "talk to the vocalist before the session starts and ask what they would like to hear from you." https://cafesaxophone.com/threads/playing-with-a-vocalist.36144/ ; https://vi-control.net/community/threads/tips-orchestrating-for-flute-and-alto-sax.50488/ (secondary)

## 5. Vocabulary (use these words in the prompt)

- swell: crescendo from silence on a held note/chord (volume pedal, bow, or fader). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volume_swell
- pad: "a long sustaining note or chord" under everything (Owsinski, above).
- stab / hit: "a single staccato note or chord that adds dramatic punctuation... usually occur on a 1-beat"; horn stab, orchestra hit. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stab_(music)
- chank / skank / chink: choked, percussive off-beat (reggae) or backbeat (funk/soul) chord strum. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chanking
- comp: "the chords, rhythms, and countermelodies" used to support a soloist or melody. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comping_(jazz)
- ostinato / riff / vamp: "a repeated melodic or rhythmic figure." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostinato
- motif / hook: short recurring idea; the hook is the motif the listener remembers.
- answer phrase / call-and-response: a response motive after the vocal call; "a Fill element as an answer to the Lead" (Owsinski). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Call_and_response_(music)
- pickup / anacrusis: notes "which precede the first downbeat" of a phrase. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anacrusis
- turnaround: "a passage at the end of a section which leads to the next section." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turnaround_(music)
- pedal tone / pedal point: "a sustained tone, typically in the bass," while harmony moves above. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedal_point
- drone: sustained note or chord backdrop through the changes.
- walk-up / walk-down: stepwise bass or chord-tone line into the next chord.
- cascade: a figure falling through successive voices/registers (horn "pushes and cascades"; the "Riders" Rhodes).
- glissando / gliss / smear: "a glide from one pitch to another." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glissando
- harmonic / chime: bell-like overtone from touching a node (steel: palm harmonics).
- ghost note: "a rhythmic value, but no discernible pitch." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghost_note
- push: anticipate the downbeat by an 8th; diamond: strike and hold (NNS); tremolo: rapid repeat of one note; vibrato; chop: backbeat percussive chord (fiddle/mandolin); double-stop; chicken pickin'; Leslie slow/fast; behind-the-nut bend; violining (= swell); long bows; pizzicato; staccato; fall / doit / shake (horns); unison vs harmonized; drop-2; countermelody / descant; oblique / contrary motion; head arrangement.

## 6. Famous single-part examples

1. ## Decision rules

1. Listen through once without playing; log key, tempo/subdivision, form, and where the vocal breathes before choosing a note. (Carlton; Nashville Number System)
2. Name the role before the instrument: Foundation, Pad, Rhythm, Lead, or Fill; never exceed four elements at once in the densest section. (Owsinski)
3. Build the part off the vocal melody and lyric, not the chord chart. (Jamerson; Porcaro; Cropper)
4. While the singer sings, leave their register and rhythm: long notes, low volume, or nothing. (fiddlehangout; cafesaxophone; harmonicalessons)
5. Fills live in the breaths: four beats max, done before the next vocal phrase starts; one fill instrument at a time. (artistworks; fiddlehangout)
6. One idea per part; if it can't be described in one line it is two parts. Bring an optional second figure, not a busier first one. (Hey; Reggie Young)
7. Earn the entrance: hold the new part until verse 2, pre-chorus, or chorus 2; leave the first chorus room to grow. (toughtones; sunopromptpro)
8. Add or remove one element per section; the last chorus gets the doubled hook or countermelody. (songen; secretsofsongwriting; violetrecording)
9. Move against the vocal: long when it is busy, active when it is static; prefer contrary or oblique motion. (secretsofsongwriting; LANDR)
10. Choose register by what is unowned: above guitar chords, below the vocal, never in the bass's lane. (Berklee; proaudiofiles; pianote)
11. Fewer notes with great tone and time beat chops; "one good shot in the right place." (Hood; Blaine; Gadd)
12. Use the idiom's signature move as a hook, not a constant: swell, chank, stab, chop, Leslie switch, once per section. (musicradar B3; hornarrangements)
13. Leave space so something can happen in it; "take a breath." (Carlton)
14. Feel beats correctness: the slightly-behind, first-take part often makes the record. (Kooper; Garcia/Nash; Blaine)
15. Bring an idea, not a clone: "if we wanted Emmons or Day, we'd hire them." (Grady Martin via Lloyd Green)
