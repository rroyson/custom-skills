# Lyric Craft

Rules applied while drafting and reviewing. Read fully before writing. Genre notes (§9) override the defaults where they conflict.

## 1. Specifics come from the user
- A song is one person, one place, one time of day, one object, one consequence. If the request gives none of these, ask before writing.
- Never invent stock "specific" props. These are second-generation AI tells: cold coffee, a coffee cup by the sink, polaroids, collarbones, duct tape, halos, dusty boots, wet pavement, streetlights, porch lights, rooftops, tangled sheets, "<month> plus weather" similes ("like a warm August breeze"), opening the song by waking up and looking out a window, singing about the song's own genre.
- Specific in verses, broader and singable in the chorus. Never a hyper-specific line next to a stock line in the same section; the stock line exposes the other.
- Contractions, slang, a swear word if the narrator would say it. AI lyrics are sanitized and "literary"; humans are candid.

## 2. Point of view, chosen before the first line
| POV | Use for | Trap |
|---|---|---|
| 1st-person narrative | a story the narrator lived | needs something the listener can use, or it's well-written whining |
| direct address (I to you) | most pop; intimate or accusing | the most overused; "you" must be a specific person |
| 2nd-person narrative | telling "you" what you can't see about yourself | |
| 3rd person | character study, observer distance (folk, country) | withhold judgment until the last verse |

Default to first person; AI lyrics avoid "I". Use another row only when the request or the story asks for it. POV and tense stay fixed inside a section. Shift only between sections and only on purpose.

## 3. Section jobs
- **Verse:** a small moment, in scene. Action, concrete noun, one detail (a brand, a number, a time, a place). Verse 2 answers "then what happened?" with information verse 1 didn't have. A verse 2 that restates verse 1 is the most common failure.
- **Pre-chorus:** two to four short lines, tension, an incomplete pattern that points at the chorus.
- **Chorus:** the big idea in plainer words, the title on the first or last line, four to six lines. Same text every time; the last chorus may change one or two non-hook lines.
- **Bridge:** a departure. New angle, new POV, new time, the twist, the thing unsaid. A rhyme scheme unlike verse and chorus.
- **Title:** reach it inside the first minute in commercial genres. Interrogate it (who, what, when, where, why, how) and pick one that changes color verse to verse. Bar test: said aloud in a bar, does a stranger turn their head?
- **Palette:** list ten nouns and ten verbs that belong to the title's world before drafting; pull from them so verses lead to the title without saying it.

## 4. Prosody: structure carries the feeling
Decide per section: stable (resolved, certain, settled) or unstable (unresolved, longing, angry, unsure).

| Lever | Stable | Unstable |
|---|---|---|
| line count | even (4, 6, 8) | odd (3, 5) |
| line length | equal | last line shorter or longer |
| rhyme scheme | AABB, ABAB | ABBA, XXAA, ABCB, ABAB with slant |
| rhyme type | perfect, family | assonance, consonance |
| line ending | stressed syllable | unstressed syllable |

A broken-heart verse in four equal AABB perfect-rhyme lines sounds like reading facts off a card. Match the tools to the feeling. Consistent endings (stressed or unstressed) within a section; switch between sections.

## 5. Rhyme
- Types, strongest to weakest: perfect (fire/desire), family (mud/truck), additive/subtractive (eve/believe), assonance (read/cheap), consonance (scene/when). Strong types close a line; weak types leave it open.
- Vary the scheme between sections. Verse, chorus, and bridge each get their own. Never AABB in both verse and chorus. Never one scheme for the whole song.
- Vary the rhyming vowel between verse and chorus.
- Slant rhyme at least two or three times per song. Perfect rhyme everywhere reads as nursery rhyme.
- Internal rhyme adds momentum in long lines; hip-hop requires it.
- Never let the rhyme pick the thought. Test: delete the rhyme word; if the meaning collapses, the rhyme wrote the line.
- No inversions for rhyme ("the night so cold"). No self-rhymes. Avoid the tell pairs in words.txt. Put the obscure rhyme word first and the common one second.
- End lines on rhymable words; rearrange the sentence instead of forcing the rhyme.

## 6. Syllables and singability
- Within a section, lines stay within two syllables of each other. Between sections, change the count: verses 7–9, choruses 4–8, pre-chorus 4–7. Hip-hop and spoken word are exempt.
- Stressed syllables on stressed beats. Nouns, verbs, adjectives carry stress; articles, prepositions, auxiliaries don't. Read every line aloud at tempo.
- Held notes want open vowels (ah, oh, ay, oo), not ee or ih. Monosyllables sing. Consonant clusters ("sixth street's strict") don't.
- Leave breath. A line with no natural pause gets rushed. Don't fill every beat.
- Verbs over forms of "to be". Cut throat-clearing ("well, I think that maybe").

## 7. Structural AI tells, rewrite on sight
- Every section the same scheme and line length. Four 8-syllable AABB lines everywhere.
- Every line end-stopped, every rhyme perfect, no slant, no unrhymed line.
- Chorus opens "can you feel it". Song resolves on forever, together, or a moral summary ("and now I know").
- Three abstract nouns in a row. "It's not X, it's Y" (unless it is the user's own quote). Stacked "like a" similes. Personified clocks, cities, and silence.
- Emotion named instead of shown ("I was so lost" instead of what you did). Maximum emotion in every line, nothing earned.
- No proper nouns, no time, no place, no "I", no slang, no profanity, no humor.
- Cosmic and elemental wallpaper: stars, fire, sky, rain, light, storm, ashes, wings, veins.

## 8. Review questions, every line before output
1. Could this line sit in any other song unchanged? Rewrite it.
2. Who is speaking, where, and what can the listener sense?
3. What did the feeling make the speaker do?
4. Did the rhyme choose the thought?
5. Can a singer deliver it: stress, breath, landing vowel?
6. Does verse 2 add information verse 1 didn't have?
7. Would an actor know what to do with this line without being told the emotion?

## 9. Genre notes
- **Country:** scene, object, a twist in the bridge or last verse; hook inside 30 seconds; ABAB or AABB with mostly perfect rhyme is native; conversational contractions.
- **Folk / Americana:** through-composed allowed (five to seven verses, no chorus); ABCB ballad stanza; a refrain that gains meaning each return; place as a character; present tense.
- **Hip-hop:** 16 bars is 8 written lines; multisyllabic and internal rhyme mandatory; couplets for bangers, ABAB for story, AXAA to breathe; break the scheme on the last bar to transition; candid, profane, first person.
- **Pop:** chorus inside 30 seconds; short chorus lines; XAXA conversational verses; near rhymes preferred; if it sounds crafted it fails.
- **Rock / indie:** imagery and energy over rhyme; quiet verse, loud chorus; 8–10 syllable lines.
- **Punk:** four-line verses, two- to four-line shoutable chorus, half-rhymes fine, one slogan yelled; done in 2:30.
- **Metal / industrial:** tactile objects, short clauses, a yellable title; rhyme optional; delete any abstract noun a physical object can replace.
- **Electronic:** the drop is the peak; one verse, a repeated phrase for a hook, no bridge.
- **R&B / soul:** leave space for runs; emotion first; an outro vamp is native.
- **Blues:** AAB three-line form, never broken.
- **Avant-garde / art:** any of the above can be broken on purpose; break one thing at a time and keep the syllable discipline inside each section so Suno can still sing it.
